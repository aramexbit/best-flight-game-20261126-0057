A bright daytime flight game: fly a propeller tourer through 12 glowing rings over a tropical archipelago, then land on the runway of the big eastern island — racing four AI rivals. Three.js, fully offline — no network requests, no assets to download. Version 17 is the LIVE-PLAYTEST FIX: the v16 live playtest found the plane turning right on its own (the storm gust's DC bias folded into the stick killed auto-level — fixed by zero-mean gusts and a raw-stick auto-level gate), the chase camera too far and too loose (now 11 m back, FOV 62, tighter tracking, less roll), the player's own crash showing no explosion (it explodes now), shadows invisible (darker, bigger, sun-offset capped), the ring arrow lying (now computed in screen space), the sea's square edge visible (the sea is 12000 m wide and dissolves in the haze), and rivals rarely seen (the blast now comes to the player with a chase boost, scripted crashes reposition ahead of the player, and showcase flybys sweep the view). The title rules are dark navy #132238 on a light pill, readable in any sky, per the user's instruction. Version 16 is the STORM BATTLE: a tornado, slanting rain, lightning and flak turn the daytime race into a battle (no weapons, no night) — explosions chain, a damaged rival dives burning into the sea, and the collision is player-relative so a keyboard player flying straight still sees the blast. Version 14 is the race STAYING IN VIEW: all four rivals start ahead of the player (50/35/25/15 m) and faster, so the player chases the pack and sees them the whole run; the mid-air collision is no longer a fixed clock at a fixed ring — KUNING and HIJAU converge on a point tied to the player's live progress, hold there orbiting, and collide only once the player closes in, so the blast goes off in front of the player, in view. The blast is a classic explosion now (big orange fireball, twenty dark smoke puffs, 28 debris shards, small faint shockwave) and both airframes are torn in two, front and rear halves tumbling away burning and smoking for 8 s; a HUD flash, a boom and a camera shake announce it. The cover freezes 1.5 s after the blast so the split halves and fresh smoke fill the frame ahead of the player. Every ring is clearance-checked to sit at least 20 m above the terrain (the user's playtest found ring 3 buried in a hillside; Gemini's fix prompt asks for 18–25 m). Landing after all 12 rings now shows "RACE COMPLETE!" with fireworks over the runway, confetti, a fanfare and a results panel (position, score, time). ## How to play

- **W/S** pitch, **A/D** roll, **Q/E** rudder, **Shift/Ctrl** throttle.
- **C** switches chase / cockpit view, **M** mutes, **P** pauses, **R** restarts.
- Centre a ring for a bonus and a streak. Watch the fuel gauge — an empty tank means gliding. Fly too slow and the wing stalls: nose down, throttle up.
- Land slow, level and on the strip for the landing bonus and a time bonus.

## Features

- Arcade flight model with thrust, drag, climb cost, stall and banked turns.
- Seeded procedural archipelago: 9 islands, ridged heightmap terrain with a dry-grass patchwork and a graded dirt apron around the runway, turquoise shallows with reef lines and a speckled water surface on a 12000 m sea that dissolves in the haze instead of ending in a square edge, drifting stylised cumulus clusters (taller multi-lobe towers with a rounded billowing base and a bumpy top — no flat skirt discs), midday sun and haze. A storm cell sits beside the mid-course rings: a spinning tornado and a smaller waterspout over the sea, a dark storm wall, 350 slanting rain streaks, cloud-to-sea lightning, flak bursts in the outer corridor and gusts that rock the plane and sway the palms. Clustered 3D trees, palms, boulders and shrubs — every trunk snapped to the rendered ground and grounded by a soft shadow disc — plus dense jungle canopies, clustered coastal cliffs and a soft sand-to-grass shoreline. A strict clear zone keeps every tree, bush and rock off the runway strip and the approach cone. Two mountain ridges give the horizon its silhouette: one across the strait past the far threshold, one along the southern shore — each a terrain-hugging range with summits, saddles, a sharp crest spine, colour blotches and rocky crest tors, colours fading toward the horizon blue. `?seed=N` rebuilds the same world.
- 12-ring course planned from the runway backwards, with a pulsing next-ring indicator, thick golden rings and sparkle particles orbiting the next gate.
- Full HUD: altitude (ft), speed (kts), heading compass strip, attitude ball with pitch ladder, fuel and throttle bars, timer, score, ring counter, race position (POS x/5) — deep navy panels with cyan glow outlines and a gold top accent.
- Procedural Web Audio: engine drone, wind, ring chimes, landing chord, flyby whoosh — no audio files.
- Chase and cockpit cameras — the cockpit is a real 3D molded cabin with labelled dials (ticks, numbers, redline), a big steering wheel on a shrouded column with a throttle quadrant, a thin glareshield brow with a red pinstripe, a curved canopy bow with struts and glazing, a three-piece headliner roof with ribs meeting the taller side walls, and side windows as real cutouts in solid door panels — title / pause / game-over screens, best score in localStorage. The airframe is a Cessna 208-style bush plane: boxy cabin with four windows a side and a red cheatline, high wing with stronger dihedral, thicker roots and bigger red tips, red struts, shorter wider fin with a red cap, wide stabiliser, longer tapered tail and a fixed tricycle gear, with the prop disc and red spinner visible from the chase camera. A big soft blob shadow follows the plane over land and sea.
- The runway island is a working airfield: full markings, tyre marks and a speckled asphalt surface, symmetric mesh-snapped sand shoulders with white edge lines on both sides, flush light fixtures (bigger domes) plus five approach lights on the extended centreline, a dirt taxiway on both sides, an apron, a hangar, a fuel shed with drums, a taller windsock, a perimeter fence, a pickup truck and a terminal hut, ringed by dense trees.
- Deterministic screenshot scenes: `?shot=start|play|cockpit|landing&seed=N`.

## Run it

Serve locally with `node serve.ts`, then open http://localhost:8080.

Screenshots are produced deterministically from the game itself with `node shots.mjs` — it serves the game, flies the course with a scripted autopilot, and writes four JPEGs to `screenshots/`.
